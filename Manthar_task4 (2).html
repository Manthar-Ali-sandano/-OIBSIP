A mind ahead of its time

The architect of the modern electric age.

While his rivals raced to profit from electricity, Tesla was already imagining a future where power travelled through the air itself — free for anyone to receive. His alternating-current system became the backbone of the world's grid, and his name became the unit of magnetic flux density.

Biography

A life spent chasing lightning.

Nikola Tesla was born on the night of a thunderstorm in 1856, in the village of Smiljan, then part of the Austrian Empire. From his earliest years he was obsessed with electricity, mechanics, and the strange beauty of numbers. He studied engineering in Graz and Prague, worked briefly for a telephone exchange in Budapest, and in 1884 boarded a ship for New York with little more than a notebook and a handful of poems in his pocket.

In America, Tesla briefly worked for Thomas Edison before the two parted ways over a dispute about direct versus alternating current. Tesla's AC induction motor and polyphase system won the day, powering the 1893 Chicago World's Fair and convincing George Westinghouse to back the technology. The result was the Niagara Falls power plant, which lit Buffalo and proved that electricity could be sent over vast distances.

But Tesla's ambition reached further. He experimented with wireless transmission, built remote-controlled boats, and dreamed of a global system of free energy. His Colorado Springs experiments produced artificial lightning that could be seen miles away. Yet funding eventually dried up, and his later years were spent in relative obscurity in New York hotel rooms, feeding the pigeons in Bryant Park.

He died in 1943, alone and nearly forgotten. But the world he imagined had already begun to take shape. Every time we plug into a wall socket, listen to a radio, or use wireless technology, we are living inside Tesla's vision. His legacy is not just a set of inventions — it is a way of thinking: fearless, generous, and utterly convinced that the future could be better.

Key Achievements

A brief chronology of a remarkable life.

  • 1888 · The AC Motor

    Patents for the Alternating Current System

    Tesla received patents for his AC induction motor and transformer, which became the foundation of the modern electrical grid.

  • 1891 · Tesla Coil

    Invention of the Tesla Coil

    He developed the resonant transformer circuit known as the Tesla coil, still used today in radio and television technology.

  • 1893 · World's Fair

    Lighting the Chicago World's Fair

    Tesla and Westinghouse introduced the world to alternating current by lighting the entire fairgrounds with AC power.

  • 1895 · Niagara Falls

    The Niagara Falls Power Plant

    Tesla's AC system was installed at Niagara Falls, proving that electricity could be transmitted efficiently over long distances.

  • 1899 · Colorado Springs

    Wireless Power Experiments

    Tesla conducted experiments in Colorado Springs, creating massive artificial lightning and exploring wireless energy transmission.

  • 1943 · Legacy

    The Unit of Magnetic Flux

    The International Electrotechnical Commission named the unit of magnetic flux density the "tesla" in his honor.
